Honest questions

Stuff students ask before signing up

Is $50/week actually realistic where I live?

In most college towns, yes — we've validated it across 47 ZIP codes near major US universities using current Aldi/Walmart pricing. In high-cost cities (NYC, SF, Boston) you'll usually land between $52–$58 with the same plan. The app shows you the live total before you commit so there are no surprises.

What if I can barely cook?

The default difficulty assumes you've boiled water and that's it. Recipes are 4 ingredients or fewer on weekdays, with a "show me what each step looks like" photo mode. About 60% of our users said they couldn't cook before signing up.

I have allergies / I'm vegetarian / I keep halal. Can it handle that?

Yes. During setup you flag allergies, dietary rules (vegetarian, vegan, halal, kosher, pescatarian), and ingredients you flat-out hate (looking at you, cilantro). We never plan around them.

Does it work with EBT / SNAP?

Yes. You can mark your store as EBT-eligible and PlateMath will only suggest items SNAP covers (no hot prepared foods, no vitamins). Walmart and Aldi both accept EBT online — we link to their portals from the grocery list.

I share a kitchen with 4 strangers. Is that going to work?

That's basically our default user. Every plan ships with a "fridge-real-estate" estimate (in shoebox units, seriously) and recipes that don't require leaving things to simmer for an hour. You can also flag "no oven access" and it'll route around it.

Who built this?

PlateMath is made by two former RAs and a registered dietitian (Anna, who ran a dining program at a Midwest state school for 9 years). We started it after watching freshmen eat Pop-Tarts for dinner three nights in a row.
